# Play-test: the descent, 2026-10-08

An agent played Cottage of the Breeze at a phone size and a desktop size,
walked the town, walked through the door on `[1,1]`, and went as deep into
the generated descent as the game would let a hero go. This report finds and
ranks. **It fixes nothing**: no pack file, no test and no other doc was
changed to answer anything below.

Everything is judged against the soul Rylee confirmed on 2026-10-08 —
"curious, low-bandwidth exploring; the thrill of opening something and
finding something fun; a cozy storybook world; no reflexes and nothing to
memorize; 30 minutes that turns into four hours" — and a change that makes
the game bigger but not more *this* is not an improvement.

## What is fun (keep this)

### F1. The walk-and-fight loop, at both sizes, all green

**What happened.** The shipped bot played the whole authored route with
keys only — cottage → town → floors 1, 2, 3 → beat the Bailiff moth, the
Warden elder and the Marshal beetle → beat the Cellar King → open the
sealed door → read the note in the King's room → see the end card → walk
back to town. **Every check passed at 1280×720 and at 390×844**:
1714 keypresses, all nine sound cues fired, all four walk directions, 18
of 25 album stickers, zero console errors, zero failed requests, no
horizontal scroll.

**How it felt.** This is the thing Rylee already named as a win in
2026-10-01 — "the wander around and fight things loop feels VERY castle
of the winds to me, which I consider a win" — and it survives contact
with a headless browser on a phone. The moment that carries it is small
and free: *"You hit a loud rustle for 5. a loud rustle falls. You earn 5
experience. You are stronger. Level 5!"* One line, one kill, one number
that went up.

**Evidence.** `logs/bot-desktop.json` (`"ok": true`, `"the whole route
completed", "ok": true, "detail": "1714 steps"`), `logs/bot-phone.json`
(`"ok": true`), `screenshots/bot-phone/fight-floor-3-moths-1.png`.

### F2. Finding a book is still the best beat in the game

**What happened.** *"You found a book: A Candle and a Note. Open Books from
the menu."*

**How it felt.** Exactly the thrill Rylee described — opening something and
finding something. It fires on the authored floors without being asked
for, and it costs nothing.

**Evidence.** `screenshots/bot-phone/fight-floor-3-moths-1.png`; the same
line in `logs/descent-desktop-town-door-seed-breeze.txt`.

### F3. The cottage and the town are cozy and they read instantly

**What happened.** Standing in her own cottage: a painted rug, the cat,
a book lying on the floor, a stone-and-wood shell, and one big legible
button that says **GO THROUGH THE DOOR**.

**How it felt.** The storybook world Rylee asked for, in the first ten
seconds, with nothing to memorize. This is the opposite of the descent,
and the contrast is the finding.

**Evidence.** `screenshots/town-cottage-desktop.png`,
`screenshots/bot-phone/01-town.png`.

### F4. "Start a new descent" re-rolls the whole run in one click

**What happened.** Menu → Descent → one button → the hero is on a brand
new floor 1 with a brand new layout, and the menu says
`Run 4 of the descent (breeze/run-3)`.

**How it felt.** The cheapest thrill in the game, and it works. It is the
"what's down there this time" pull, and it is the strongest single idea
the generated descent has.

**Evidence.** `logs/descent-desktop-seed-run3-sneak.txt` (`NEW_DESCENT_DOC[2]`
→ `"seed": "breeze/run-3"`).

## What is boring

### B1. A generated floor has nothing in it to find

**What happened.** Every generated floor reached, on every seed, at both
sizes: `floor_items=0`, `bag=[]`. The map legend has exactly four
glyphs — `#`, `.`, `u`, `d`. There is no chest, no book, no treasure, no
secret. The hero can walk a floor top to bottom and have nothing to show
for it.

**How it felt.** Dead. This is the direct opposite of the soul's "the
thrill of opening something and finding something fun", and of the
"Collections" answer Rylee gave for what pulls her deeper. There is
literally no collection to start.

**Evidence.** `logs/descent-desktop-seed-run1.txt`, `-run2.txt`,
`-run3-sneak.txt` and `logs/descent-phone-seed-run3-sneak.txt`, all four
`FLOOR depth=` lines ending `floor_items=0 bag=[]`.

### B2. Every floor is the same four creatures at the same numbers

**What happened.** On every seed, on both sizes, `foe_hp=[4, 5]` and
`foe_atk=[1, 2]` — every monster on the floor. Identifiers are `m0`,
`m1`, … `m34`: no name variety, no stat variety, no family variety beyond
the four the Section lists. Floors 1 and 2 are indistinguishable.

**How it felt.** A corridor of the same animal. The descent declares nine
floors; it plays like two.

**Evidence.** the same four `FLOOR` lines, `foe_hp=[4, 5] foe_atk=[1, 2]`.

### B3. The number of monsters on floor 1 swings from 20 to 35 for no reason a player can feel

**What happened.** Floor 1 monster count is 20, 28 and 35 across the three
seeds — driven purely by how many rooms the floor draw happened to make
(602, 855, 1065 walkable tiles). The Section sets no `mobs` key at all;
the count is engine policy derived from floor area.

**How it felt.** Random difficulty on the first floor of the run, before
the player has learned anything. It is not the good kind of random.

**Evidence.** `FLOOR depth=0 … enemies=20 walkable=602` /
`enemies=28 walkable=855` / `enemies=35 walkable=1065` in the three seed
logs.

### B4. On a phone, a generated floor is one lit dot in a black band

**What happened.** At 390×844 the arrival view of `cellar-0-1` shows a lit
patch roughly two tiles across, with the hero as a single orange pixel,
inside a black rectangle that runs from about a third of the way down the
screen to two thirds, with parchment above and below. You can see
approximately four tiles of game.

**How it felt.** Not a game. Not even a cave — a smudge. This is the
low-bandwidth device Rylee named as the target, and on the descent it
shows the least.

**Evidence.** `screenshots/descent-seed-run3-floor1-phone.png`,
`screenshots/descent-seed-run3-floor2-phone.png`.

### B5. The generated floors look like everyone else's dungeon

**What happened.** The generated floors draw `dungeon-wall`,
`dungeon-floor`, `dungeon-stairs-up` and `dungeon-stairs-down` — the
engine's built-in legend — over a `#0d0f12` near-black background. The
pack's `sections/cellar.json` names `".": "cellar-flagstone"` and that
name is never used. Compare the same hero on an authored floor.

**How it felt.** Generic. The authored cellar has a painted flagstone
floor you can read at a glance; the generated one has a colour. "Change
by section" (Rylee, 2026-10-08) cannot happen while there is one legend
for the whole descent.

**Evidence.** `screenshots/descent-seed-run3-floor1-desktop.png` beside
`screenshots/authored-cellar-floor2-desktop.png`; the ignored `tiles` is
engine limit **EL-2**.

## What is confusing

### C1. The door on town `[1,1]` does not lead to the descent, and nothing says so

**What happened.** `world.json` declares
`descent.entry = {region: town, at: [1,1]}`, and `acts/act-1/world.json`
declares an authored transition on the same tile. At play time there are
**two** transitions out of `town` `[1,1]`, and the authored one is first in
the list, so it wins. Walking through the door puts the hero in the
authored cellar. Walking back up out of the descent puts her on that same
tile, one step from falling into the authored cellar again. The validator
prints `ok`.

**How it felt.** As a player: there is a door in the corner of town, I walk
through it, and I am somewhere else entirely, and the game is happy about
it. As a creator: the pack declares a descent, the menu offers a descent,
and the descent has a door in the town that does not go there — with no
warning anywhere.

**Evidence.** `logs/descent-desktop-town-door-seed-breeze.txt`:

```
AT_ENTRY town [1, 1]
AFTER_ENTRY_REGION floor-1 Walk into a monster to strike it. …
AFTER_ENTRY_TRANSITIONS … {"from": "town", "at": [1, 1], "to": "floor-1", "to_at": [3, 3]}
                            {"from": "town", "at": [1, 1], "to": "cellar-0-1", "to_at": [27, 34]}
```

**Rank: 1.** This is the finding that decides whether the descent exists.

### C2. The way-ahead hint is always about "floor-1", everywhere

**What happened.** `#combat-live` reads *"To reach floor-1, go north-west."*
in the cottage, on the town door tile, in the town, and on floor 1 and
floor 2 of the generated descent — which are not `floor-1` and have no
name the player has ever been given. `vefr look` prints it verbatim in
town.

**How it felt.** The one line the game uses to point you somewhere is
pointing at the wrong place with a name that does not exist in this mode.

**Evidence.** `screenshots/vefr-look-descent-attempt-desktop.png` (the
overlay text listing includes `combat-live  To reach floor-1, go
north-west.`); `logs/descent-desktop-town-door-seed-breeze.txt`
`AFTER_ENTRY_REGION` line; `screenshots/descent-seed-run3-floor1-phone.png`.

### C3. Arriving anywhere greets you with the way *out*

**What happened.** On arrival at a generated floor the hero stands on the
up-stair anchor, so the big button reads **GO UP THE STAIRS**, the point
of interest is *"the stair up"*, and the hint points north-west. The way
down is somewhere else on a 73×55 map you cannot see.

**How it felt.** The first thing the game offers a player who has just
come down is a way back up. It is a small joke and it is not funny when
you cannot find the stairs.

**Evidence.** `screenshots/descent-seed-run1-floor1-desktop.png`,
`logs/arrival-camera-and-verb-row.txt`.

### C4. On arrival in any dungeon floor the hero is drawn underneath the Messages panel

**What happened.** Walk through the town door into `floor-1`. The map is
drawn in the top-left of the play area and the hero — at tile `[3,3]` —
lands behind the "Walk into a monster to strike it" toast. She is not on
screen. Walk four tiles down and she is. The big button also reads a bare

**How it felt.** You walk through the door and the game appears to have

## What is missing

### M1. A reason to go down at all

Twenty to thirty-five monsters, zero items, zero chests, zero books, on
every seed. The soul's pull is *finding something fun*; the descent
currently offers *being hit by something*. This is the gap that makes
the descent boring rather than merely rough, and it is bigger than any
individual bug below.

### M2. Any healing anywhere in the descent

The bag is empty on arrival on every floor and no floor carries a potion.
The temple in town is the only way back to full health, and the only way
back to town is up every stair you came down. "Rising" danger (Rylee,
2026-10-08) means gentle near the top — this is the opposite: the first
floor is where the hero dies.

### M3. Floors 3 through 9 are unreachable

The Section declares 9 floors with a `pattern` that puts an `infested` and
a `hub` special on floors 4 and 7, a `landing` on floor 5 and the Cellar King
warden on floor 9.
Across four seeds, at two sizes, playing carefully, **the deepest any run
reached was floor 2**, and only once, at 1/6 health. So the specials, the
warden, and every mood change that would come with depth are untested and
unplayable today.

**Evidence.** `logs/descent-desktop-seed-run3-sneak.txt`:
`FLOOR depth=1 region=cellar-0-2 … hp=1/6` then
`DESCEND_FAILED depth=1 region=cellar-0-2 could not reach the stair (left)`.

### M4. A way back up that is not a staircase

`recordLanding` is defined (`397-the-descent.js:824`) and exported
(`:1202`) and called from nowhere: no control anywhere offers it.
Going down four floors means climbing four floors in a 6-health hero.
PLAN.md's elevator rule is not reachable by a player.

### M5. Anything pointing at the door to the descent

There is no point of interest, no label and no hint on the town tile at
`[1,1]`. A player who does not read `world.json` will never find it.

### EL-1 — a transition on the descent entry tile silently shadows the descent

**Reproduction.** Pack: `Rylee-Bee/cottage-of-the-breeze` at `c1b645a`.


`world.json` declares

```json
             "entry": {"region": "town", "at": [1, 1]},
             "sections": ["cellar"] }
```

`acts/act-1/world.json` declares `{"from": "town", "at": [1,1], "to": "floor-1", "to_at": [3,3]}`.

At play time `window.VEFR_TRANSITIONS` holds both, authored first:

```
{"from": "town", "at": [1,1], "to": "floor-1",   "to_at": [3,3]}
{"from": "town", "at": [1,1], "to": "cellar-0-1","to_at": [27,34]}
```

Walking onto `town [1,1]` and pressing Interact goes to `floor-1`. The
descent is unreachable by walking. `397-the-descent.js:678 pushTransition`
only skips an *identical* `(from, at, to)` triple, so the authored entry
is never replaced, and `maplab.descent_errors` never compares
`descent.entry.at` against the act's transitions.

**What Cottage needs.** Either a validator error naming the collision, or
a rule that the descent's own entry wins the tile. Right now the only way
for a creator to find out is to play the game.

**Acceptance criteria a test could check.**
- `vefr check --pack` on a pack whose `descent.entry.at` collides with an
  authored transition exits non-zero and prints one sentence naming the
  tile and both regions.
- With that pack woven, walking to the entry tile and pressing Interact
  enters the generated floor named by `locate(1)`.

**Blocks:** Cottage content work items 1 and 5; the whole descent.

- A pack with no descent ignores the parameter exactly as it does today.

**Blocks:** any repeatable per-seed review; CI coverage of the descent.
