## The premise, in one sentence

You live in the cottage at the end of the road because nobody else wanted it,
there is a stair in the corner of the kitchen, and you keep going down because
somebody should.

**Why you go down, and it is a small reason.** You are not brave. You are not
chosen. Nobody sent you. The stair is *in your own kitchen*, so it is not a
quest, it is just there when you make tea. Under the door there is a note in a
hand you don't know: *you keep going down. someone should.* You are not doing
it for the town. You are doing it because the town is quiet and cold and grey,
the Spell Keeper keeps a cake back for whoever comes up the stair, and you would
very much like to be the one who is glad to see. It turns into the biggest thing
you have ever done without ever once feeling like a hero about it.

**What you are carrying up is heat.** Not a relic, not a crown. The town has a
cold hearth and does not know why. Everything below is a piece of the town's own
fire, brought home.

## The town

Small. One road. The cottage, the tavern, the Trader's stall, a temple you wake
up in, and the black gap where a building used to stand. People are polite and
nobody will say the thing.

### Who is there, and what each of them wants

| Who | Wants | What is in the way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Spell Keeper** | For her to be *named*, eventually, as one of the ones who went down. She has known how this ends since before you arrived. | The town has decided she is a shopkeeper. Nobody asks her a real question. |
| **The Tavern Regular** | To hear the truth about the fire told out loud, in the tavern, with people in it. He sat at the same table for twenty years. | He was a boy when The Breeze burned, and he has decided not to have been a coward about it. |
| **The Dwarf Smith** | Work. He is the best mender in the district and there is nothing left to mend. | His forge is banked by three and cold by four, every day, and he will not say why he does it early. |
| **The Fisher** | A full net, and his brother Oren home. Oren went down once and did not come up, and the town has been gentle about it in a way that clearly means *ask no questions*. | The river runs warmer and thinner every year. He blames the weather. He does not quite believe it. |
| **The Gardener** | Something to grow that isn't food. She talks to the beans and means it. | Every bed is thinner than the year before. She has started giving seedlings away to people who don't want them. |
| **The Trader** | A full stall. He buys what you bring up and asks no questions at all, which is a kindness. | He is the only person in town who is not wary, because he never asks. |
| **The Hearth Cat** | The quiet to go on being quiet. She owns the cottage. You live here at her pleasure. | She has decided about you, and we do not find out what. |

### What the town is losing, slowly

**The fire.** Not a metaphor — an actual thing. The old tavern's fire was the
town's fire. It warmed the smith, and the bread, and the beds of anyone with
nowhere to sleep. When The Breeze burned, the fire went with it, and nobody
rebuilt a fire hot enough to matter. It is not that the town is dying. It is
that the town has gone *cold and quiet*, in the specific way of a house where
nobody wants to be the first to speak.

Every day costs a little more: a colder forge, a smaller catch, a thinner bed,
a road nobody walks after dark. Nobody is cruel. The town is **wary of anyone who
goes down**, which is the stupidest possible thing to be wary of, because
everything warm in this story is coming up from below with somebody's hands on it.

## The sections

Floors are cut by `seed + depth`, not fixed numbers, so these are written as
sections: each one is a *place*, with its own mood, its own animals, its own
guardian, and one answer.

### Section 1 — The Cellar Under the Cottage

**The place.** Flagstone, damp brick, a well-mouth in the corner that nobody in
town admits is a well. Someone cut this stair before the cottage was built, and
the cottage was built around the top of it. The further down you go, the older
the stonework gets, and then the mortar runs out and it is just rock somebody
carved steps into.

**The mood.** Close and familiar. Colder than home, but the *shape* of home —
somebody put a shelf here once. You are not far below the kitchen you had tea
in. It is the gentlest place in the game and the first one you are not afraid
of. Danger is barely a word down here. Rats, moths, a spider in a good corner.

**The guardian.** The **Cellar King** — a very large crowned rat who has been
holding a stair shut with a seal for longer than the cottage has stood. It is
not clever and it is not cruel. It is doing a job someone gave it a long time
ago and it is tired. *(His name is not chosen. That one is Rylee's.)*

**The vault.** A ring of old red wax, a small chest, and a note folded on top of
the gems — *"Nothing down here was ever as unkind as the sealing… Kindness is
lighter than it looks, and it knows the way home."* (That note is already written
and already in the pack. It does not change.)

**But read it again at the bottom and it does.** In here it sounds like someone
who knows. At the end it will read like someone who has been down this whole
time, saying the only true thing they can, in the dark. That is the cellar's
whole job: it states the thesis in a dead voice, before you know a single name.

Also down here, already in the pack: the letter in the chest, tied with thread,
signed with a name you don't know — *I went down on purpose, and I meant to come
back. Tell the keeper I kept my word about the cakes.*

### Section 2 — The Long Well

**The place.** Under the cellar the rock opens out into something dug on
purpose: a shaft the width of a barn, going down further than a well should,
with cisterns cut into the walls at intervals and little stone staircases
spiralling between them. Water somewhere far below, not falling — just *there*,
moving very slowly, like something breathing in its sleep. Everything echoes
once and then does not echo again.

**The mood.** Wet, listening, enormous. Still cold, but the cold of a riverbank
at night rather than a cellar. You feel small and you are fine. Danger rises
here for the first time and it rises as *water* — things that wait and then are
simply closer than they were.

**The guardian.** The **Longneck** — a great grey heron, older than the town,
standing on a ledge in the middle of the shaft with its wings half up. It will
not move and it will not attack you. It is not guarding treasure. It has been
standing between the town's water and everything else since before the sealing,
and it will still be doing it after you leave. Its feathers are a lovely pale
grey and it has never once been in a hurry.

**The vault.** Behind the Longneck's ledge, a room that is not a room: a
**bread oven**, cold, and beside it a ledger in oilcloth. Old Merrin's ledger —
he kept The Breeze, and he wrote down everything the fire needed, in a tidy
shopkeeper's hand. Its last page is the answer to the first thing this town
will not say.

**What you read:** *"Nineteen of us agreed. A fire is only a fire. It does not
care what it is over, only that it burns. Merrin of The Breeze will hold the
room. He has a dog and a kettle and he can be relied on."*
Underneath, in a different, shaking hand: *"He was not to be relied on."*

**The answer.** The fire was set on purpose. By the town. To hold down a room.

### Section 3 — The Banked Fire

**The place.** The stair stops going down and starts going *in*, sideways, into
the flank of something vast and hollow. Chimneys. Soot furring the ceiling like
the roof of a chimney nobody ever came down. Fire-brick, laid by someone who
knew what they were doing. There is heat — real heat, the good kind — and it is
the first warm place under the ground. At the warm end of it, where the passage
turns, there is the sealed door.

**The mood.** Ember-orange, sooty, slow. *Rest.* The first floor group in the
game that feels like somewhere you could sit down, and that is exactly when
things start to hurt. This is where "Rising" stops being a suggestion:
everything down here lives in the warm and is comfortable.

**The guardian.** **Old Longsleep** — a vast soft beast, more barrel than
animal, curled on the warm floor and asleep. It has kept the heat for a hundred
years by lying on it. It guards nothing. It is a hearth that learned to breathe
and was never told to stop.

**The vault.** A chest built into the wall, in front of the fire, so warm that
the hinges are the coldest thing in the game. Inside: gems, a bar of old silver,
a **coal that will not go out**, and under it, a note.

**What you read:** *"If you are reading this you have got this far, and you are
warm, and I am glad. I am not going to be able to bring it up. I am going to go
back down for him. Take the coal. Do not let the town put the fire out again.
Tell them it was never the room's fault."*

**The answer.** Somebody went down on purpose to bring the fire back up, meant to
come back, and went back down instead for somebody else. The letter in the
cellar is hers. So is the coal.

### Section 4 — The Blue Door

**The place.** The sealed door at the warm end of the passage. It is an ordinary
cottage door, painted blue once, with a dog bowl beside it that has been full of
weather for forty years. **The coal from section 3 opens it** — the fire opens
the door that was built to hold it in — and then there is the room the town
sealed over.

It is **unburnt**. That is the whole reveal and it takes one second. Everyone in
town said there was a fire down here and there was — but the fire was over the
*stair*, in the passage, and never on the room itself. Two beds, both made. A
kettle on the hob. A table laid for six. Bread on the table that is not forty
years old.

**The mood.** Still, waiting, and unbearably kind. No horror register anywhere:
the deepest floor of the game is a room where someone set a table. The danger
is one tired animal and the size of your own feeling.

**The guardian.** **Bramble** — the old tavern's dog, a yellow mutt gone grey
around the muzzle, sitting in the exact doorway he was told to sit in. He is not
guarding the room. He has been in the doorway for four decades because a man he
loved went through it and never came back, and somebody had to keep the door. He
does not attack you. He gets up, once, and moves aside, and he looks at the
stairs behind you.

**The vault.** The room itself. There is no chest. The vault is the place, and
what is in it is: the table; a second blanket folded at the foot of the bed; and
Old Merrin's coat on a hook, forty years dry.

**What you read:** nothing. There is no note in the vault. The note was in the
cellar, written by somebody who came down before you, and everything from here is
a thing you can *pick up and carry*, which is the better part.

**The answer.** What the town sealed away was its own kindness. They were not
protecting themselves from something under the room. They were protecting
themselves from finding out what they had done.

## What each depth reveals

Escalates by *place*, never by volume, and never into horror.

1. **The cellar** — this is older than the house. Something is held down, and someone down here approves of it.
2. **The Long Well** — the town set the fire. Nineteen people signed for it, and one of them owned the room.
3. **The Banked Fire** — someone went down to undo it, on purpose, and did not finish. She is alive. She is in town.
4. **The Blue Door** — it was a room with two beds and a table laid for six, and the fire was never on it. The town pulled out its own hearth because it was afraid of being kind.

Each answer arrives twice, in different clothes: once as a thing you read, and
once as a resident's line that is different now that you know. Nobody carries a
flag. Miss the line and you lose a line, not progress. Every note stays in your
Library forever, re-readable, at your pace.

## The final resolve

You take the coal up. That is the whole mechanical resolve and it is deliberately
small: one warm thing in your bag, and the stairs home are long.

When you come up into the cottage the **hearth-cat** is on the rug, and the
hearth is lit, and you did not light it. The Dwarf Smith takes the coal to his
forge, and his forge is the first properly warm thing in this town. The Tavern
Regular finally asks the question he has been not asking for twenty years, out
loud, and the room goes quiet, and then people answer.

**And the Spell Keeper.** Carry her own letter up to her — no marker, no
counters, nothing owed; she simply has one new line the moment you have read it
— and she goes still, and then: *"I wrote that on the ninth step. I wasn't
brave either."* She is the one who went down on purpose. She came back. She has
been waiting forty years for somebody else to go down too.

The cottage gets its lit window. The town is a little warmer. Nobody fights a
final villain because there was never one — there was a town, and a fire, and a
room.

## The part-2 door

The cheapest door that fits, and the one I am asking Rylee to judge the whole
story on:

> **Under the hearth-slate in the kind room there is a second stair, and it is
> warm, and nobody in this town has ever once mentioned it.**

Bramble will look at it when you take him up. That is the only omen. Nothing
forces it, nothing names it, and a part 2 needs no new machinery at all — it is
the same stair again, one section further, with a different room at the bottom.
